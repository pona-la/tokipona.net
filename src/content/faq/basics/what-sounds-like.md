title: What does Toki Pona sound like?
---

Toki Pona is composed of 14 of the
[most common sounds across all the languages](https://phoible.org/parameters).
There are nine consonants (p, t, k, m, n, s, w, and j), and five vowels (a, e,
i, o, and u).

All the consonants are pronounced the way they would be in English, except for
j, which is pronounced like a y. The vowels are pronounced as they are in most
languages with a five vowel system (a like "father," e like "bed," i like ee in
"feet," o like "soda," and u like oo in "food").

The exact pronounciation is variable and may depend on where a speaker is from.
If you'd like to hear samples of Toki Pona, try the
[kalama sin podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WweF4seKYX4&list=PLjOmpMyMxd8Qs2mAXcLk817tQy_AQj09u&index=3),
which features a range of different speakers.

#### But what _language_ does Toki Pona sound like?

Toki Pona has its own unique sound, and proficient speakers don't tend to equate
it with a different language. According to
[a study by Paolo Coluzzi](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Paolo-Coluzzi/publication/382980583_Esperanto_Klingon_and_Toki_Pona_evaluating_non-speaker_perceptions_of_the_orthographic_and_phonological_characteristics_of_three_popular_constructed_languages/links/6920fc37a130337711be360a/Esperanto-Klingon-and-Toki-Pona-evaluating-non-speaker-perceptions-of-the-orthographic-and-phonological-characteristics-of-three-popular-constructed-languages.pdf),
the perception of what natural languages Toki Pona sounds like is dependent on
perspective. To many Americans, Toki Pona sounds like Japanese, but folks from a
Malaysian background are more likely to think that Toki Pona sounds like
Italian. None of these perceptions are objective.
